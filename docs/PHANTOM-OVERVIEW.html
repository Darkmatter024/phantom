PHANTOMField intelligence for data center technicians

One app in a technician's pocket that knows what rack they're at, what phase it's in, and what to do next.

PHANTOM is an offline-first mobile app for rack-and-stack deployment. A technician loads the site's Master file, taps a rack, and works it through five phases with every check, scan, photo, and note recorded against that rack — then hands the shift off with nothing lost.

It runs in Safari on an iPhone, needs no install beyond "add to home screen," and works with gloves on in a cold aisle with no signal.

What a technician actually does

The same four moves, from the technician's side. Tap counts are the design target the app is being held to.

Arrive, load the Master

Open the app. If the site's Master isn't loaded, the first screen says exactly one thing: Load a Master to start. If it is, the screen says how many racks are on site and tells you to tap one.

1 tap after the name gate

Find the rack

Search by ID, browse the list, open the floor map, walk the 3D aisle, or scan the rack's label. Flagged and blocked racks are marked. Your assigned racks sit on top.

≤ 2 taps to any rack

Work the rack

The rack screen holds everything: phase pipeline, checklist, devices with U positions, scan, photo, log note, assign, QR, blockers, and the tools that apply to this rack right now.

0 navigation — it's all here

Hand it off

Set the shift end and the countdown runs. When you close, the handoff carries every rack's state forward. Nothing lives only in someone's head.

≤ 2 taps to hand off

Everything the app does

Grouped by where each feature lives, because that's how PHANTOM is organized: the Master is the source of truth, the rack is the unit of work, and a short list of things sit above the rack.

The Master

The site's deployment spreadsheet, ingested once. Every count, rack, device and U position in the app traces back to it.

Single source of truth
  • Spreadsheet ingestionLoads the site Master (.xlsx) and turns it into racks, devices, positions and port maps on the phone.
  • One Master per siteLoading a new one is an explicit replace with a confirmation — no silent overwrites, no two Masters fighting.
  • Cached for offlineRestored from the phone's storage on launch, with the saved date shown so a stale Master is never mistaken for a fresh one.
  • PIN-protected replace and deleteSwapping a wrong Master for the corrected one takes seconds — protected by a PIN, not by being hard to find.
  • Multi-site awareEach Master carries its site; the header always shows which site you're working.
  • Reconciles everywhereA device count on one screen must match the same count on every other. Disagreement is treated as a defect, not a display quirk.

The rack

Tap one rack and every check, action, and piece of evidence for it is on that screen. Tools appear only when they apply to this rack.

The unit of work
  • Five-phase pipelineMechanical → Power → Network → Compute → Validation, with a per-phase checklist and progress bar. The rack's status is the sum of its phases.
  • Gates and honest overridesA phase can be force-completed when the floor demands it — but "gate override active" stays visible and the bypass is logged with the technician's name.
  • Next actionDerived from the rack's current phase, on the device, offline: "Continue network." One line, always correct.
  • Devices with U positionsEvery device in the rack listed with its U range, top of rack first. Unknown positions show as unknown — never guessed.
  • ScanScan a device or rack label. A scan that doesn't match what the Master expects records a mismatch even if the technician taps pass.
  • Photo evidenceCapture photos against the rack; a per-rack gallery keeps them with the work, not in the camera roll.
  • Log note, assign, QRNotes credited to the technician, rack assignment, and a QR for the rack — all on the rack screen.
  • Blockers and discrepanciesLog what stopped work and where. Blocked racks are counted at fleet level and flagged in every rack view.
  • Long-phase warningIf a phase has run far longer than it should, the rack says so — with the real elapsed time and no invented "typical" figure.
  • Paste anythingDrop raw text from a switch, a BOM, an elevation, or an EDP and the app parses it into the rack's port map or component list, on the phone.
  • Port map, BOM, manifest, rack mapRack-scoped views of what's expected versus what's been done, read from the Master.
  • Optics, audits, power, burndownOperational tools that render for the rack you're on: optic ledger, audit checks, power readiness, remaining work.
  • ISOLATEA guided down-link wizard for troubleshooting: eight steps, permission tiers, and an escalation packet built deterministically from what the app already knows.
  • Activity logEvery state change on the rack, timestamped and credited: "John: POWER: pending → in_progress."

Seeing the site

Views for finding a rack and understanding the floor. The picture lives here; the work stays on the rack.

Survey views

  • Floor mapRows and positions laid out as the floor is.
  • 3D aisleWalk the aisle, tap a rack to focus it, see racked-versus-pending and flags on the chassis. Pick up to five racks to compare.
  • Rack inspectionFront and rear views of a single rack with an explode option and ghosted empty U slots.
  • Fleet pipelineSite-level view of which phase the deployment's leading edge is in, racks done, and blockers.

Intelligence

An assistant that already knows the site, the rack, the phase, and the devices — so a question in the aisle gets a specific answer.

Online only, and honest about it
  • DCT AssistantAsk by voice or text. Answers stream back with the rack's live context already included. Attach a ticket to ask about it.
  • Offline-awareThe app is offline-first; the assistant isn't. Its door is visibly dimmed when there's no signal, so nobody waits on a spinner in the cage.
  • Parsing on the phoneBOM, port map, EDP and CLI parsing run locally — no network required to make sense of pasted text.
  • Secured through a proxyNo API key in the app. Calls go through a Cloudflare Worker locked to PHANTOM's own domain and capped per month.

Shift and handoff

The few things that sit above any one rack: the clock, the handoff, and the site controls.

Above the rack
  • Shift end and countdownSet it once; the app counts down. Off until you set it — no fake timers.
  • HandoffEverything the shift recorded rolls forward. The incoming technician sees "In progress: s1:001 — Network" on their first screen.
  • Acceptance and historySign-off flow and the full change history for the deployment.
  • Pause deploymentA single fleet-level control for stopping work site-wide.
  • System panelMaster, profile, diagnostics, and exit — one short list, one place.

Built for the cold aisle

Design rules the whole app is held to, because it's used with gloves, in noise, under harsh light, often with no signal.

Platform
  • Offline-firstEverything except the assistant works with no connection. Data lives on the phone and survives relaunch.
  • No installIt's a web app: open the link in Safari, add to home screen. Updates arrive on the next launch.
  • Glove-safe targetsEvery control is at least 44 points. No precision taps, no tiny close buttons.
  • Data honestyNo fabricated telemetry, no decorative scores, no "typical" numbers that were never measured. If the app doesn't know, it says so.
  • Multi-tab guardOpening the app in a second tab warns you before edits can collide.
  • Phone and desktop shellsThe phone is the product; a wider layout serves the desk.

How it's kept safe to change

A single developer shipping to a tool people rely on. The process is built so nothing reaches a technician's phone unverified.

Engineering discipline
  • Verified on device before releaseEvery version is checked on an iPhone by the owner before it's promoted. A build the phone hasn't passed cannot ship.
  • Owner-only release gateLive serves from a release branch only the owner can promote. The verified stamp is enforced by a hook that no automation can bypass — and it records failures, not just passes.
  • One visible change per versionSmall, surgical edits against verified source. If something breaks, the cause is one ship wide.
  • Automated tests on iPhone WebKitBrowser tests run in an iPhone emulation before the human check. Tests are mutation-checked: a test that can't fail is removed.
  • Code knowledge graphThe codebase is mapped before any edit so changes are made against real anchors, not assumptions.
  • Review agentsAutomated reviewers for edit surgery, data honesty, cold-aisle usability, storage shape, and report fidelity — advisory, never a substitute for the device check.

Three rules that shape every decision

Written down, enforced in review, and the reason the app keeps getting simpler rather than bigger.

The rack is the unit of work

Load Master → pick rack → work the rack. Anything that isn't about the rack in front of you moves onto the rack or gets removed.

A gauge that lies is worse than no gauge

Real numbers or an honest "not set." No scores for unset state, no invented baselines, no button that pretends there's a job when there isn't.

Every door must earn its place

Two paths to the same fact is a defect. A change that opens a door has to close one. The app is measured by how few taps a stranger needs.
